Extraction is the skill that turns an Active Matter raid into progress. Winning a firefight is only one step; the reward matters only after you leave the unstable zone. The official game description emphasizes dangerous raids for loot and PvP battles, so the right question is not “Can we take one more fight?” but “What does one more fight buy us?”

This guide uses a decision framework that remains useful while the launch balance changes. It intentionally avoids claiming fixed extraction timers, map names, or loot values that may change between the current build and release.

## The extraction decision in three checkpoints

Use three checkpoints during every raid.

### Checkpoint one: the objective

Ask whether the objective is complete, partially complete, or no longer realistic. If it is complete and the squad has a valuable item, extraction becomes the default. If it is incomplete, decide whether the next interaction is reachable without crossing an exposed route.

Do not let a nearly complete objective force a bad push. “Almost done” is not a resource; it is only a reason to compare the remaining cost.

### Checkpoint two: the kit

Count ammunition, healing, armor condition, and utility. A squad with full health but no ammunition may be less safe than a squad with moderate damage and a clear route. Treat every missing resource as a future decision you may not be able to solve inside the raid.

If one player is carrying all the healing or the only useful utility, that player should not lead every doorway. Protect the resource that keeps the squad alive.

### Checkpoint three: the route

Point to the extraction route before moving. If nobody can explain the next two pieces of cover, stop and reorient. A route you cannot describe is a route you are likely to cross while distracted.

When possible, leave through a route that reduces decisions: cover, one direction of travel, and a fallback if contact begins. The safest route is not always the shortest one.

## A practical risk score

You can use a quick four-question score after every major action:

| Question | Safer answer | Warning answer |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Complete | Requires another exposed interaction |
| Resources | Enough for one fight and the exit | Low ammunition or no healing |
| Squad | Together with clear roles | Split or carrying a downed teammate |
| Route | Known cover and fallback | Unknown lane or repeated contact |

If three answers are in the warning column, leave. If two are warnings, move toward extraction and avoid optional fights. If zero or one is a warning, you may have room for another objective — but only if the reward is worth the added risk.

## After winning a fight

Winning a fight creates a dangerous false sense of safety. Perform a reset before looting:

1. Secure the immediate approach.
2. Confirm that every teammate can move.
3. Reload and recover only from cover.
4. Pick the highest-value items first.
5. Mark the extraction route.

Do not stand over the loot while discussing what to keep. One teammate should watch the approach while the others sort. If the area cannot be held, take the item you came for and move.

## Squad communication that helps

Use short calls that change behavior: “objective complete,” “two magazines,” “armor damaged,” “extraction next,” or “fight optional.” Avoid vague calls such as “we are fine” when the squad has not checked its resources.

Assign one player to make the default extraction call. This is not a permanent leader role; it simply prevents four people from assuming someone else has decided. Anyone can veto the route when they see a new threat.

## When greed is correct

Leaving early is not always right. Take another objective when the route is known, the reward changes what you can afford, and the squad still has enough resources to survive a bad engagement. The key is that the extra objective must improve the next decision, not merely increase a number in storage.

If you decide to continue, set an exit trigger before you move: a resource threshold, an enemy contact, a damaged teammate, or a time/route marker. A pre-agreed trigger makes it easier to leave without arguing after the risk has already increased.
