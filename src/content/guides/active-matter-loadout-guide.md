The best Active Matter starter loadout is not the most expensive kit. It is the kit you can replace after a bad raid and use confidently in the next one. Until the launch build settles, think in jobs rather than a fixed weapon tier list: reliable primary damage, enough ammunition for one objective, basic survivability, and a bag with room for the trip home.

The official game description frames Active Matter around raids, loot, and PvP in unstable zones. That makes loadout discipline more important than a single “best gun” claim. A weapon that wins one duel but leaves you unable to finish the objective is not a good starter choice.

## The four-part starter kit

Build the kit in this order:

1. **Primary weapon:** choose the gun whose recoil and sight picture you can read. Consistent hits matter more than a theoretical damage ceiling.
2. **Matching ammunition:** bring enough for one objective and a fight, but avoid filling every slot before you know the route.
3. **Protection:** wear armor you can afford to lose. A damaged expensive item creates pressure that makes the next decision worse.
4. **Healing:** carry a recovery option and know whether it restores health, repairs protection, or treats a status effect. Read the item text in the current build.

Add utility only after these four jobs are covered. A useful grenade or tool can open a route, but it should not replace ammunition or healing on your first attempts.

## Budget rules for new players

Use a simple replacement rule: after every successful extraction, keep one complete backup kit in reserve. If you cannot afford that backup, downgrade the next loadout before you queue.

Do not judge a kit by the value shown in your stash. Judge it by how many raids it can support. A cheap kit that extracts three times may teach you more and produce more resources than a premium kit lost in one ambitious push.

Separate “raid money” from “learning money.” Raid money pays for ordinary attempts. Learning money is the small amount you are willing to spend testing a new weapon, route, or setting. When learning money is gone, return to the baseline kit instead of escalating.

## Ammunition checks

The most common ammunition error is carrying several types without a plan. Before you leave the staging area, verify that your primary and spare ammunition match. In the raid, reload during safe pauses rather than after you have already taken contact.

After each engagement, ask:

- How much ammunition remains?
- Can this amount finish the chosen objective?
- Is the next fight optional?

If the answer to the second question is no, extract or change the plan. A full bag cannot compensate for an empty magazine at the wrong time.

## Armor and healing are one decision

Treat armor and healing as a pair. Armor helps you survive the first mistake; healing determines whether you can recover before the next decision. If the current build distinguishes damage to health from damage to protection, read both item descriptions and do not assume a medkit repairs armor.

When damaged, do not heal automatically in the open. Move to cover, check whether you are being followed, then recover. In a squad, call out the reset so one teammate is watching the approach while another uses the item.

The official site has continued to publish fixes and shelter changes around the launch window. That is a reason to re-check item behavior after updates rather than treating a launch-week spreadsheet as permanent truth.

## Three starter loadouts to test

These are test templates, not official recommended builds.

### The learning kit

Use your most controllable primary, modest ammunition, basic protection, one healing option, and no expensive utility. The objective is to learn a route with low replacement cost.

### The objective kit

Keep the same primary, add enough ammunition for the required interaction and one defensive fight, then reserve space for the objective reward. This kit is for players who already know the way out.

### The squad support kit

Carry a reliable primary and healing with one utility item that helps a teammate reset or cross a dangerous lane. Agree on who carries the recovery supplies before the raid so three players do not bring the same item while the team lacks another.

Run each kit at least twice before judging it. A single death can come from route choice, timing, or a bad fight rather than from the loadout itself.

## Keep a patch-aware loadout note

Write down the build number, primary weapon, ammunition, armor, healing item, objective, and extraction result. Add one sentence about what caused the loss or success. When an update changes recoil, armor, shelter storage, or objectives, you can compare runs without relying on memory.
