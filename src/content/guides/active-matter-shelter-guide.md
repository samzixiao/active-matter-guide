The shelter is the part of Active Matter that turns successful raids into a longer-term advantage. The official site describes shelter customization, and recent official updates mention improvements such as moving the Refiner and Replicator, a “Collect All” control, and additional shelter decoration options. That makes the shelter more than a menu: it is where you organize the resources that make the next raid easier.

Because the game is approaching its September 15, 2026 Steam launch, treat exact costs and upgrade rankings as patch-sensitive. The plan below focuses on order and verification rather than inventing a fixed economy.

## First, learn what persists

Before spending materials, inspect the shelter screens and note what each object does. Separate four kinds of progress:

- items that help only the current raid;
- materials stored for future crafting;
- upgrades that change the shelter or production loop;
- cosmetic or layout choices.

Do not sell or convert a material until you know whether it is needed by an upgrade you want. If the interface shows a recipe, requirement, or queue, take a screenshot or write the name in a small patch note. This is especially useful during Early Access, when descriptions and costs can change.

## The safe upgrade order

Use this order for your first decisions:

1. **Clarity:** unlock or inspect the systems that explain storage, crafting, and collection.
2. **Recovery:** prioritize anything that helps you replace a basic raid kit or return to action after a loss.
3. **Information:** improve the part of the shelter that helps you understand items, objectives, or available options.
4. **Production:** invest in a station only when you have a repeatable supply of its inputs.
5. **Comfort and decoration:** personalize the shelter after the first four jobs are working.

This order is deliberately conservative. A production building that cannot be fed is not an upgrade; it is a locked resource pile.

## Organize materials by purpose

Keep three inventory notes: “use now,” “reserve,” and “unknown.” Unknown items should stay untouched until you have checked their recipe or the official documentation. When the shelter becomes crowded, sell or convert only from the use-now group.

After a raid, sort in the same order every time:

1. restore the baseline kit;
2. reserve materials for the next shelter goal;
3. move uncertain items into storage;
4. keep one spare recovery item;
5. review what remains.

The routine is more valuable than a perfect first guess because it reduces accidental spending when you are tired or returning from a bad run.

## Refiner and Replicator checks

Official update notes mention that the Refiner and Replicator can be moved in shelter customization and that the Replicator gained a collection control. When you use either system, confirm the current interaction in the live build rather than relying on an older guide.

For each station, record:

- input materials;
- output or purpose;
- time or queue behavior;
- whether collection is manual or grouped;
- what happens if the station is moved.

If a station consumes a material you also need for raid recovery, reserve that material first. Efficiency is not useful if the next kit cannot be funded.

## Layout for fewer mistakes

Place high-frequency stations near the path you naturally take after returning from a raid. Keep storage and crafting choices visually separate. A clear layout reduces the chance that you click a costly conversion when you only meant to check a recipe.

If you play co-op, decide whether the shelter is primarily for one player’s long-term progression or for the group’s preparation. Label that expectation in chat. A convenient building arrangement is not worth a disagreement over who owns the materials.

## A weekly shelter review

Every few sessions, review the shelter with five questions:

- Which upgrade improved a real raid decision?
- Which station has inputs sitting unused?
- Which material did I spend without understanding?
- Which item should be reserved for a future patch or quest?
- Can I replace the baseline kit after one loss?

If the answer to the last question is no, stop expanding and rebuild the reserve. A healthy shelter supports raids; it should not prevent them.
